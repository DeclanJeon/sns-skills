# Market entry: from demand to a video test

## 1. 시장조사 — find the market before choosing the concept

Set the decision (reach, repeat viewing, subscribers, or another requested outcome), the time horizon, and hard constraints actually supplied by the user. Mark inferred priorities as provisional. Unless the user locks a niche, **do not filter the search by what the channel already makes, its best upload, or what the creator likes**. An unnamed niche is not a blocker for exploratory research. If the target language/market is not clear, disclose the provisional slice rather than calling it universal.

Use [Million Index](million-index.md): without a seed, sample randomly to identify leads, then use targeted searches to test the viewer need behind promising leads. With a seed, start with audience-intent queries and at least one distinct phrasing when the call budget permits. Small-channel outliers help find opportunities, not measure the entire market. Do not claim research you could not run.

### When Million Index is unavailable

- If the user requires Million Index only, report the blocker and stop retrieval. Otherwise, disclose the fallback and use `flucto-media-research` to find public YouTube leads: up to two targeted queries of 10 results each, preserving the requested scope. With no seed, use the stated objective or market to form exploratory queries; if neither supports a meaningful query, ask one consequential question rather than invent a niche.
- Verify only what the fallback actually exposes. Unknown publication age, market, Shorts classification, or subscriber count does not pass a hard filter. List those leads separately or exclude them; do not imply that the demand scan or channel baselines ran.
- Report demand as `limited` when there is usable but incomplete evidence or `unknown` when it is missing. Offer at most one clearly provisional test if helpful. If the fallback is also unavailable, name the missing evidence and use only material already supplied; do not invent a market result.

## 2. 수요 검증 — identify what the audience came for

Write each hypothesis as **“[viewer in a situation] wants [answer, outcome, emotion or experience] and gets it when [observable payoff].”** Separate the topic or prop from what the viewer receives. Record URLs, channel IDs, publication and observation dates, public views and subscriber counts, and the inspection actually performed. Review the content/captions of shortlisted videos when extracting structure; comments are self-selected qualitative evidence when actually read. A title alone is a lead, not inspected structure.

- Validate in two places: run the [market demand scan](million-index.md#separate-outlier-and-demand-searches) with no view floor for the promising viewer need, then fetch the [channel comparison sample](million-index.md#comparison-sample-after-outlier-discovery) for shortlisted channels. A demand scan is not a channel baseline, and neither needs a 100k hit as a prerequisite. Check whether each candidate actually delivers the same payoff before comparing its views; record exclusions such as different language, unrelated topic, format, duplicate, or incompatible age. Compare similar cohorts, keeping ordinary and weak uploads. Report sample size, typical level and inclusion rules. Do not count reposts, shared source compilations, or multiple uploads by one creator as independent repeats.
- Use **repeated signal**, **limited**, **mixed**, or **unknown**. For a repeated signal, require comparable evidence from at least two independent channels; report the same-channel baselines used. If only high-performing references are available or the baseline rows are non-comparable, report a demand hypothesis (`limited` or `unknown`), not a repeated demand verdict. One hit, only mismatched windows, or a list without inspected mechanism is not a repeated mechanism. Do not treat unobserved retention or comments as facts, or claim causal proof from public views.
- Rank by demonstrated demand within the comparison; handle factual substantiation, rights, safety, ability to deliver, and fit as separate gates. If promising demand cannot be served honestly, explain why and consider the next supported opportunity rather than pretending the weaker one won the demand analysis.

### Inspection checkpoint

First use accessible public playback or captions (Flucto caption workflow if available and appropriate). Record the method and scope per reference: `playback`, `caption only`, or `metadata only`; include a segment/time anchor when available. Captions support spoken progression, not cuts or first-frame composition. If inspection fails, preserve the reason and use titles only to name candidate needs. Return an original **provisional** test if useful; do not write a verified reference pattern or claim to know what caused views.

## 3. 진입 포인트·패턴·구조 — turn the opportunity into one original concept

For the strongest testable opportunity, specify three different things:

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Entry point** | Who already wants this and in what moment? What truthful promise would bring them in? Which underserved angle or distinctive delivery can this channel actually prove, show or perform? Mark a claimed gap as a hypothesis unless investigated. |
| **Pattern** | What transferable expectation and payoff recur? Name both the observations supporting it and what remains inference. Examples: diagnose a visible mistake, reveal an unexpected cause, or watch two approaches compete. |
| **Structure** | Draft one new video: first frame/title–thumbnail promise, progression, proof or turning point, payoff, and optional next question. Apply the creator's voice, expertise, character and resources **here**. Use original examples, setting and expression; do not lift scripts or shots. An explainer, demonstration, or story can all implement this workflow. |

Choose **Shorts** if one clear question and payoff can satisfy the need in a short video and format evidence supports that test. Choose **long-form** if the payoff requires development, comparison, context or accumulating proof, and the channel can make it. If a weaker format has no comparable evidence, call it a separate hypothesis; a successful short does not validate a long version. Do not classify a reference as an official Short from duration alone.

## 4. 테스트 — design the smallest test that teaches something

State the hypothesis, the video and **one focal variable for the next iteration**, the held-constant conditions, observation window, comparison group, one primary metric and one guardrail. A brand-new concept establishes a baseline; it is not a controlled causal test. Examples: Shorts watch-choice rate with average watch percentage as guardrail, or long-form source-matched impression CTR with early retention as guardrail. If repeat viewing or conversion is the goal, specify the corresponding channel or video metric instead of defaulting to raw views. Choose the window relative to upload (e.g. first 7 days), not by calendar totals.

Default to a two-upload test for a new idea: publish the first as a baseline, then try a follow-up with the same viewer need and one changed element. State observable decision conditions before publishing, anchored to the channel's own comparable baseline; use a provisional threshold only if labeled and justified. Predeclare the decision: **repeat** when compatible comparisons consistently support the hypothesis without a guardrail drop; **revise** the diagnosed bottleneck; **hold** for thin samples, mixed signals or incompatible windows. Do not scale a series on one hit. Measure the smallest follow-up that could reverse the decision.

**Expected views, if requested:** Only use comparable first-party results from the same channel, same format, similar lengths and equal post-age, including weak and ordinary uploads. Report sample size, window, median and empirical band when defensible; label it a baseline/scenario, not a calibrated forecast. Otherwise withhold a numerical estimate and name the exact Studio data needed. Never multiply the channel baseline by a competitor's lift.

## Recommended output

1. **Market and demand verdict:** viewer need, search passes/changed filters, relevant references and rejected lookalikes, comparable sample and what does or does not repeat.
2. **Entry point → pattern → structure:** one original video proposal, format and why.
3. **Test card:** objective, variable, metric and guardrail, comparison/window, repeat/revise/hold rule. Add a view range only when defensible.
